Wheel Bearing Replacement in Birmingham

A hum or rumble that changes with road speed can come from a wheel bearing, but tyres, brakes and driveline components can make similar noises. We inspect the vehicle to identify the source before agreeing wheel bearing or hub replacement.

Find the noisy or worn component

Tell us when the noise starts, whether it changes when turning and whether you can feel vibration. We check the relevant wheel-end components rather than choosing a side from a recording alone. A bearing may be noisy without obvious looseness, while wheel play can have other causes. If the wheel feels loose, the noise worsens suddenly or handling changes, stop safely and contact us before driving further.

Bearing or complete hub replacement

Some vehicles use a separate bearing; others use a hub assembly. The registration helps identify the correct arrangement and parts. Brake components, fasteners and any wheel-speed sensor involvement are considered during the repair. Send an MOT failure report if wheel play or a bearing fault was recorded. We repair mechanical failures and advisories; MOT tests and retests take place at a testing station, not at our workshop.

Wheel noise, brakes and suspension

A rumble, knock or vibration can overlap with brake faults or suspension wear. Send the MOT failure report if the problem was found during a test.

Workshop FAQs

Before you book

Does a humming noise prove a wheel bearing has failed?

No. Tyres and other rotating components can sound similar. Inspection is needed to identify the cause and affected side.

Do you replace wheel bearings after an MOT failure?

Yes. Send the report and registration so we can assess the recorded fault and plan the repair.
