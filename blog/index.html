Practical repair advice

Before you drive with an engine fault

The symptoms and the circumstances matter. These checks help you describe the problem to the workshop and plan the right next step.

Red oil warning, knocking or sudden timing noise

A red oil-pressure warning is different from a routine service reminder. Stop safely and switch the engine off if a serious oil warning or heavy knocking develops. Continuing to run a distressed engine can increase internal damage. An oil level check can provide useful information, but a correct level does not prove that pressure is adequate. Tell the workshop when the warning appeared, whether noise accompanied it and what happened immediately beforehand. Do not repeatedly restart the engine merely to see whether the warning goes away.

Overheating and coolant loss

A temperature warning, steam or sudden coolant loss calls for a safe stop and assessment. Do not open a hot cooling system. The repair may involve a leak, circulation fault or more extensive damage; overheating alone does not prove that the head gasket is the original cause. Tell us whether the coolant level has been dropping, the heater behaviour changed or another garage recently worked on the cooling system. A pressure test and relevant mechanical checks may be needed before choosing parts or deciding whether internal engine work is required.

Non-start and fault-code reports

Describe whether the engine turns normally, clicks, cranks unusually quickly or will not turn. Those are different starting points for diagnosis. Keep any existing code report and note whether the fault is intermittent, hot or cold. A stored code identifies a detected condition; the wiring, supply, fuel, timing or mechanical cause still needs checking. Tell us about recent battery, engine or timing work and whether the vehicle stopped while moving. Send the registration and running status before arranging recovery so the initial assessment and access can be discussed.

Timing chains and wet belts are different designs

BMW N47/N57 and JLR 2.0 Ingenium enquiries often involve timing chains. Wet-belt assessment applies to particular Ford EcoBoost/EcoBlue and earlier PureTech engines. Similar names and engine capacities do not prove that the drive design is the same. A preventative belt or chain job also differs from repairing an engine after timing loss. We identify the fitted engine, consider its maintenance record and assess any associated damage before agreeing the scope. Use the service pages below to read about the relevant repair route.
